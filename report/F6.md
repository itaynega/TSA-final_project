## 6.1 What worked

**The pre-registration worked, and it is the only thing in this project that worked exactly as
designed.** Four modifications, four quantitative predictions, four abandon conditions and a
selection rule were fixed in writing and committed at `ac426c7` `[config]` before any Stage-2 run
existed; `git merge-base --is-ancestor ac426c7 HEAD` confirms every run descends from it. (`ac426c7`
is repository state, read read-only; it is an identifier, not a measurement, and is the one class of
value here that is not a fact-sheet row.) The rule was then applied without discretion, including in
the one case where discretion would have produced a better-looking report. Nothing was amended: the
frozen document carries exactly one dated `## Amendments` entry, correcting a stated parameter count,
and it altered no prediction, no threshold and no selection rule.

**Arm B confirmed all three of its registered predictions**, and the third was a prediction of a
null (fact sheet §4):

- The estimator returned **`24`** `[training]` (class 1, §4.1) from the ACF and from the periodogram
  independently, under strict integer equality — ACF peak `ac[24] = 0.8851540915365546` `[training]`

  The abandon condition, *"ACF and periodogram disagree on the dominant period"* (class 3,
  `[training]`), did not fire.

  §4.4) via an independent end-to-end `--cycle auto` retrain, and to **`0`** exactly `[validation]`
  (class 1, §4.4) on the shared W = 24 checkpoints. The declared band was **`±0.0005`**
  `[validation]` (class 3, §3 Arm B).
- The W-curve is flat. Spread across the eight per-W three-seed means:
  **`0.0015267984642975962`** `[validation]` (class 1, §4.3), against a declared **`±2σ`** band of

## 6.2 What did not work

**This is the longest subsection of F6 and that is the correct proportion.** The pre-registration
stated in advance what this outcome would be worth: *"all four arms null on all four horizons and
the W-curve flat … is a coherent, publishable-shaped result … It is not a failed project. It is the
project's actual finding, and the report should be written so that outcome needs no rescue"* (class
3, §4). What follows is written that way. Nothing below is a concession extracted after the fact.

The headline is blunt and is not softened anywhere in this report: **the rule, applied without
discretion, selected the arm that changes nothing.** Arm B's estimator returned `24` `[training]`
and `24` was the value already hard-coded, so the `--cycle auto` path produces a byte-identical
setting string and an architecturally identical model — `n_params = 661640` `[config]` (class 1,
§1.4) on both sides — and Arm B's W = 24 row re-evaluates the reconstruction's own three checkpoints
rather than retraining. The pre-registration then separately denies that arm a "win", because the
declared **`±1σ`** test (class 3, §4 item 4) is applied on the metric and horizon the arm's own
prediction named, and Arm B's prediction named **equality**. §4.2 ranks; §4.4 classifies; the frozen
text applies them independently.

### 6.2.0 Arm A degraded the model decisively, and stopped where it said it would

Arm A removed a per-window least-squares trend before instance normalisation and added it back
damped on the output. Measured at the registered H = 96 gate, `[validation]`, seed 2024, against the
reconstruction anchor **`0.6712632722155959`** `[validation]` (class 1, §2.1):

| φ | Arm A val_MSE `[validation]` | Δ vs reconstruction `[validation]` | Δ / σ_validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 | `0.6952801971213933` | `+0.024016924905797432` | **`+21.037673525385248`** |
| 1.0 | `0.9491608951697321` | `+0.2778976229541362` | **`+243.42498001392693`** |

All six values are class 1, fact sheet §7. Against the declared **`±0.002`** `[validation]` null band
(class 3, §3 Arm A) the two deltas are **`12.0`** and **`139`** times the band (class 1, §7). The
registered abandon condition fired; φ = 0.9 and φ = 0.95 were never run, no φ was ever frozen, and
Arm A never produced a three-seed mean at H = 96, so it could not be ranked. `n_params` is
**`661640`** `[config]` (class 1, §7) at both φ — the mechanism changes normalisation, not size.

Three things make this a measured outcome rather than a non-result. The arm was abandoned on a
*corrected* implementation: a real origin bug — the level carried from the window centre while the
damped trend projected from the window end — was found and fixed first. The abandonment is robust to
which reconstruction anchor is used: recomputed against the epoch-3 artefact φ = 0.8 reads
**`+7.29`** σ_validation and against the clean `reconstruction_v2` retrain **`+21.04`** σ_validation
(both class 1, §7, `[validation]`), so all three anchors clear the threshold by one to two orders of
magnitude. And the acceptance gate that let the implementation through was itself wrong in a way a
worker caught: the gate as written could not have detected a deliberate off-by-one in the damping
sum, and the test that does catch it had to be added.

**What F6 cannot say about Arm A is the interesting part.** The mechanism-level explanation — that
the in-window linear slope carries almost none of the within-window variance and is mean-reverting,
so projecting it forward is worse than projecting nothing — exists in this project **only as prose**.
The two figures that would support it live in narrative documents and in no result file, and are
barred under standing order T15′ (fact sheet, `## Withheld under T15′`, entries W1 and W2). The
qualitative claim is stated; the numbers are not printed. Arm A's *finding* does not depend on them.
**What is blocked is the explanation, not the result** — which is a precise and unusual thing for a
report to be able to say about itself.

### 6.2.1 The flat W-curve and the ablation corroborate each other — and §3 Arm B predicted the conjunction before either was measured

**This is the strongest result in the study, and it is a null.**

Two independent interventions on the same mechanism return the same answer. The **W-curve** varies
the hyperparameter that indexes the Temporal Query across the declared grid
**{6, 8, 12, 23, 24, 25, 48, 168}** `[validation]` (class 3, §3 Arm B) — a **`28`**-fold range
(class 2: `168 ÷ 6`, inputs from the declared grid) straddling the true diurnal period. The entire
spread across the eight three-seed means is **`0.0015267984642975962`** `[validation]` (class 1,
§4.3), from a minimum of **`0.6714039036970284`** at W = 6 to a maximum of **`0.672930702161326`** at
W = 12 (both class 1, §4.2), against a declared **`±2σ`** band of **`0.0022832301182749365`**
`[validation]` (class 1, §4.3) — a ratio of **`0.6687010880231152`** (class 1, §4.3). Every pair of
the eight sits inside the band. The **ablation** does not vary the mechanism; it deletes it. Arm D's
criterion drops both the Temporal Query and the channel-attention layer on a `[training]` statistic,
and the resulting model sits inside the declared **`±1σ`** band `[test]` (class 3, §4 item 4) at
three of four horizons: Δ/σ = **`−0.331`** at H = 96, **`+0.619`** at H = 336, **`+0.020`** at
H = 720 (all class 1, §3.1). ETTh1's channel count is **`7`** `[config]` (class 1, §3.6).

**A flat curve plus a firing ablation is strictly stronger than either alone, because each closes
the other's most natural escape route.** A flat W-curve on its own invites the reading that the
sweep was insensitive — that W never reached the mechanism, or that the mechanism was saturated, so
of course nothing moved. The ablation refutes that by removing the mechanism outright and measuring
the same null, which is only possible if the mechanism genuinely contributes nothing at C = 7.
Conversely, a firing ablation on its own invites the reading that the comparison was underpowered,
or that one ablated configuration was unlucky. The curve refutes that by showing the model is
indifferent across a 28-fold range of the very hyperparameter that indexes the ablated mechanism —
a far denser sampling of the null than any single on/off contrast can provide. The two results
cannot both be artefacts of the same insensitivity, because they are produced by different
interventions on different axes.

**And the conjunction was registered in advance, with its reasoning, before either half existed.**
§3 Arm B, frozen and committed at `ac426c7`: *"It follows from the ablation: if the Temporal Query
contributes nothing measurable at C = 7, then nothing indexed by W can matter either, so
W-sensitivity must vanish. If the curve is flat, two independent results agree and each becomes much
stronger. If the curve is NOT flat, the ablation conclusion is wrong and that is a more interesting
finding still."* (class 3.) That sentence is what converts the agreement from a post-hoc pattern
into evidence. A conjunction noticed after the fact is a story; a conjunction whose *alternative*
was pre-declared as *"a more interesting finding still"* is a test that could have failed and did
not. Nothing in the outcome could have been chosen after seeing it, because both outcomes had
already been priced.

**What the result licenses, stated narrowly.** It licenses: on ETTh1 at C = 7, TQNet's Temporal
Query and channel-attention layer are below this cell's noise floor, and the model's sensitivity to
the period hyperparameter W is below it too. It does **not** license any claim about the
architecture on the high-channel-count datasets where the paper makes its case; the attention map at
C = 7 is 7×7, close to the smallest non-trivial case the mechanism can be given, and this report
makes no claim beyond the cell it measured. The honest form of the finding is that **the
architecture was oversized for a seven-channel dataset**, and that reading is better supported by
this evidence than any alternative — including the one that would have been more flattering to the
work, that the modification improved something.

### 6.2.3 σ ≈ 0.002 against a claimed effect of 0.004 — and the two metrics' σ do not move together

**Most of what this study tried sits below the noise floor of the cell it was run in, and that is the
most useful thing it measured.**

The pre-registration said so before any of it ran. Its §1 records TQNet's claimed margin over
CycleNet at H = 96 as **`0.004`** MSE `[test]` (class 3, §1) and its own noise floor as
**`0.00215`** `[test]` (class 3, §4 item 4), with the consequence spelled out: *"The bar is our
σ = 0.00215, not the paper's 0.001. Our noise floor is already half the size of the entire effect
TQNet is famous for."* The measured value came in at **`0.0021541981747125473`** `[test]` (class 1,
§5), so the declared figure was accurate to **`0.0000041981747125473`** (class 2:
`0.0021541981747125473 − 0.00215`). The claimed effect is **`1.8568393785468478`** measured σ
(class 2: `0.004 ÷ 0.0021541981747125473`) — under two standard deviations of seed-to-seed variation.
A cell in which the headline effect of the paper is under 2σ of its own reproduction noise can
resolve almost nothing smaller, and four of this study's arms proposed things smaller.

**This is a statement about the cell, not about the arms.** It was chosen before the arms were
designed, it was declared in the pre-registration's first section, and it bounds every verdict
downstream: the confirmations in §6.1 are confirmations of *predicted nulls*, which is the only kind
of prediction this noise floor can decide cleanly.

**The second half of the point is that σ is not one number.** `report/horizon_sigma.md`, via fact
sheet §5, measures **two σ per horizon** — one for MSE, one for MAE — and they do not move together
(all class 1, `[test]`):

| H | σ(MSE) `[test]` | σ(MAE) `[test]` |
|---|---|---|
| 96 | `0.0021541981747125473` | `0.0003752412844557897` |
| 192 | `0.0008376947093460351` — a dip | `0.0015920913669081958` — a rise |
| 336 | `0.004789584272220202` | `0.001368805006375168` — a dip |
| 720 | `0.022769078789010144` | `0.01486937531963135` |

Across the single step from H = 96 to H = 192, σ(MSE) **falls** by a factor of
**`2.5715790617733156`** (class 2: `0.0021541981747125473 ÷ 0.0008376947093460351`) while σ(MAE)
**rises** by a factor of **`4.2428470236615805`** (class 2:
`0.0015920913669081958 ÷ 0.0003752412844557897`). They move in opposite directions over the same
step, and their ordering inverts between adjacent horizons. Neither is monotonic in the horizon.

**Three concrete failures this would have produced, and one it did produce.** A single-σ table would
have judged MAE deltas at H = 192 against a σ that is a factor of four too small, turning "no
measurable effect" into a "win" for a difference that never left the noise. Reusing the H = 96 σ at
H = 720 would have been wrong by roughly an order of magnitude. Assuming σ grows with the horizon —
the natural assumption, and the one the pre-registration explicitly refused to make — would have been
wrong at the first step. And the failure it *did* produce is the one in §6.2.2: Arm D's H = 192
falsification clears 1σ because H = 192 carries the **smallest** of the four σ(MSE), and that fact is
a property of the measurement, not of the arm.

**The registered rule that made this survivable** is §1.1's substitution clause — *"the per-horizon σ
measured in P3 replaces 0.00215 in every threshold below"* (class 3) — written for the horizon axis
before anyone knew a split axis would also be needed. It is the reason two of Arm A's horizons are
recorded as `unevaluable` rather than as nulls: the effect each predicted is smaller than the σ later
measured for that horizon, so the prediction is untestable as written, which is a registered
consequence rather than a discovered excuse.

### 6.2.4 The arm64 platform replication — post-hoc by construction, and deliberately not folded in

Every Stage-2 number in this report was measured on x86. The three live arms were later re-measured
on the macOS/arm64 machine, and `report/platform_arm64.md` records the result. Two facts from it,
and nothing else:

- The two data-derived switches are deterministic functions of the CSV and return the **same answer
  on both platforms**: Arm B's period estimate agrees at 24/24 by ACF and periodogram on arm64 as on
  x86, and Arm D's criterion returns `drop` on both. The arm64 ranking on the pre-registered
  endpoint reproduces the x86 ranking. **The selection is not a platform artefact.**
- The platform contribution to the H = 96 test mean is of order **1e-10** for both live arms —
  `1.55e-07` and `4.17e-07` of the x86 seed sd for Arm B and Arm D respectively (class 1, named
  source row, `report/platform_arm64.md`, `[test]`). Against a seed σ of
  **`0.0021541981747125473`** `[test]` (class 1, fact sheet §5), platform is invisible.

**And that is exactly why none of those measurements may be substituted into this report.** The
pre-registration's §4 item 5 forbids post-hoc promotion of a result, and `tools/horizon_sigma.py`
(LF-normalised sha256 `9247501d11d36244110a0b0a536e8cd6922a85a6ffad9080ac10021e2aa89d08`) enforces
the exclusion **in code**, not in prose: its selection predicate is
`arm == "reconstruction" and git_commit == "9663bcd"`, so the arm64 records cannot reach the σ every
Stage-2 verdict is judged against. Reversing that in a discussion section would make a post-hoc
measurement into a reported result by the back door — the code would still exclude it and the report
would silently include it. **No arm's value is restated here from that file**, and the fact sheet's
own `# Amendments` block opens by declaring the same restraint for the same reason.

**The finding worth carrying forward is not about platforms at all.** The same file records that
repeat trainings of the *full* model on one machine, same seed, same environment, produce **different
weights and different predictions** — largest per-element prediction difference `2.384e-06`
`[config]` (class 1, named source row) — while repeat trainings of the *pure MLP* variant reproduce
**byte-identically**. The attention path is the only structural difference between the two groups and
it is the one that fails to reproduce. Two full-model runs with different weights printed a
bit-identical MSE from the upstream metric. **A metric match is not a hash match** — demonstrated, on
this data, with the very metric anyone would have used to check. §6.2.7 is the incident this project
paid for want of that sentence.

### 6.2.6 The CRLF traceability failure — every digest in the fact sheet is machine-local

**The project's traceability rule identifies a file by its path and its sha256. On this repository,
that pair does not identify a file.**

`.gitattributes` sets `* text=auto`, with `*.csv text`, `*.sh text eol=lf`, and `*.pdf` / `*.png`
marked binary. Git therefore stores LF and converts on checkout, so a text file's bytes on the x86
Windows working copy are not the bytes the same commit produces on Linux or macOS. Measured
read-only tonight, on this clone:

```
git --no-optional-locks ls-files | while IFS= read -r f; do [ -f "$f" ] || continue;
  a=$(sha256sum < "$f" | cut -d' ' -f1); b=$(tr -d '\r' < "$f" | sha256sum | cut -d' ' -f1);
  [ "$a" != "$b" ] && echo "$f"; done | wc -l
```

**236 of the 265 tracked files present differ between their raw digest and their LF-normalised
digest** (class 2, repository state, `[config]`). Among them are all **90** tracked
`results/**/*.json` records — every one of them differs (class 2, repository state, `[config]`).

The worked example, and it is the file F5's paper column depends on:

| Form | Digest of `TQNet/result_authors_reference.txt` |
|---|---|
| raw, x86 working copy (CRLF) | `0325a5b0939d2c4b373693d2603105759f1a518160ec6695f9760d86cc6dca84` |
| LF-normalised (what arm64 sees) | `5264c43d52262f1ac267637f209ab54c5ffe8472a1bdcd75eaf41c78fe2f3040` |
| git blob (`git hash-object`) | `f1312442079b1892c3f48ea3e376c1462726d431` |

All three `[config]`, class 2 (repository state). **One committed blob, two working-copy digests.**
The platform-independent identity of a tracked text file is the **git blob hash**; what the fact
sheet records is a **working-copy digest**, and a grader cloning this repository on Linux or macOS
computes a different value for every one of those 236 files.

**Sharper still, and this is the part a reader will find on their own: the fact sheet is internally
mixed.** §2.1 pins `results/validation/validation_metrics.log` at
`4af37f4fe4be4fcc01fb17e30a15db0b06c3b390fc764d22d205e0b4bb9b573d` `[config]` (class 1, §2.1), which
is the **x86 raw** digest — its LF-normalised digest is different. Amendment A1 pins
`TQNet/result_authors_reference.txt` at `5264c43d…` `[config]` (class 1, Amendment A1), which is the
**LF-normalised** digest — its x86 raw digest is different (D13 above). The two
digests were recorded by two writers on two clones, and both are correct on the machine that recorded
them. Amendment A3 already raises this for one gitignored checkpoint path and calls it *"the only
exception"* on the grounds that *"every other hash in this sheet is of a committed file and is
therefore clone-independent."* **That premise is false for every tracked text file**, which is the
larger form of the same defect and is disclosed here rather than corrected, because §§0–11 have a
single writer.

**The choice made, and why.** The alternative was a global `eol=lf` normalisation, which would
rewrite 236 files and invalidate every digest recorded in `report/factsheet.md`,
`report/selection.md`, `report/w_curve.md` and `report/platform_arm64.md` — hours before submission,
in the one part of the project whose entire purpose is that recorded digests match. **Disclosure was
chosen over renormalisation.** The rule that follows from it is stated once, here: where this project
pins a digest of a text file, the digest to use is the **LF-normalised** one,
`tr -d '\r' < FILE | sha256sum`, and a raw mismatch on a text file on the x86 clone is expected and
is not grounds to halt.

**One consequence in the other direction, recorded because it is a limit on the same claim.** No
`.csv` file is tracked at all — `*.csv text` in `.gitattributes` applies to nothing, and
`TQNet/dataset/ETTh1.csv` is excluded by `.gitignore`'s `dataset/` rule (class 2, repository state,
`[config]`: `git ls-files '*.csv' | wc -l` returns **0**). The dataset's identity therefore travels
not as a tracked file but as the `data_sha256` field
`f18de3ad269cef59bb07b5438d79bb3042d3be49bdeecf01c1cd6d29695ee066` `[config]` (class 1, §6) asserted
inside every run record — which, because that file is LF on both clones, is the one digest in this
project that is platform-independent by accident rather than by design.

### 6.2.7 The checkpoint incident — a trajectory match mistaken for a hash match

**One of `26` protected checkpoints was overwritten by a command whose only purpose was to verify
that nothing had changed.** (`26` is `[config]`, a named source row:
`STAGE2_WORKPLAN_2026-08-09.md` §7j, LF-normalised sha256
`cfbd14a1cbe445b0845d78c7495dfe644a5e439955c6f2fe27f8cbd4bdcbbe77`.)

The command reused the exact setting string of an existing checkpoint and was piped through
`head -20` to avoid waiting for a full run. `head` truncates displayed output, not the underlying
process: training continued to epoch 3 before the pipe closed, and early stopping's save-on-improvement
overwrote `checkpoint.pth` with epoch-3 weights. The repair re-ran the identical seeded command to
completion and was judged successful because **every epoch's train and validation loss matched a
reference trajectory**. It was the wrong reference — the smoke run, not the original — and the match
was eyeballed, not hashed. The weights the repair produced differ in sha256 from both.

The damage was silent because the artefact is a faithful measurement of a broken model. The
regenerated sidecar reports **`0.6869550701723053`** `[validation]` (class 1, §2), which is not an
error message, not a NaN and not an outlier by inspection. What identified it was corroboration, not
suspicion:

| Value | What it is | Deviation from the anchor |
|---|---|---|
| `0.6712632722155959` | The anchor — logged five times with a `SANITY ANCHOR` line before the incident, in a log that carries both states of the same directory over time | — |
| `0.6712632718563285` | Dedicated clean retrain, `reconstruction_v2` | `−3.6e-10` |
| `0.6712632724477633` | Independent `--cycle auto` retrain | `+2.3e-10` |
| `0.6869550703100449` | An independently `head`-truncated run — corroborating **the artefact**, not the anchor | agrees with the artefact to `1.4e-10` |

All class 1, fact sheet §2.1, all `[validation]`. Three runs trained to completion agree to under
`4e-10`; the truncated pair agree with each other and disagree with all three by roughly `0.0157` —
about eight orders of magnitude larger. The correct three-seed mean is **`0.6724990175677814`**
`[validation]` (class 1, §1.1).

**The incident flipped a result, which is how much it mattered.** Computed with the poisoned cell,
the W-curve spread is **`0.006325713189656135`** `[validation]` — **`2.77` × 2σ**. Computed correctly
it is **`0.0015267984642975962`** `[validation]` — **`0.6687010880231152`** × 2σ (all class 1, §4.3).
The two fall on
**opposite sides of the 2σ line**, so the poisoned reading would have falsified §3 Arm B's flatness
prediction and destroyed the corroboration §6.2.1 is built on. A single overwritten file, undetected,
would have reversed the study's strongest finding.

**Two things were deliberately not cleaned up.** The epoch-3 checkpoint remains on disk (sha256
`c5d0f7bbc057d48608c15e60a4872712b363a5fa4c12238ba77fb16860773ca2` `[config]`, class 1, §2), and
`results/validation/_summary.json` (sha256
`d6f77703d135cea199a028b496ed210f2e1bfa1a341f03589b1f68b27dd3dbbc`, class 1, §2) **still carries the
poisoned value** and does not contain the true one — a `201,494`-byte roll-up of all `50` sidecars
(both `[config]`, class 1, §2 and §10) that
looks authoritative, is the file a reader is most likely to open first, and is barred as a source for
every number in this report. Neither is deleted, because this project's norm is that a superseded
artefact is retained and marked. Both are evidence, and both are live hazards; whether
`_summary.json` ships, and with what header, is an open decision recorded in the fact sheet's §11.

**Standing order 14 is where this ended up**, and it is stated in the incident's own terms: no
command that can train into, delete or modify an existing checkpoint, result file or dataset artefact
runs without a sha256 before/after inventory of every file it could plausibly touch — not just the
ones it is supposed to touch — in the same dispatch, with the diff showing only intended changes. Its
final clause is the sentence the repair needed and did not have: **a trajectory match is not a hash
match.** It cannot be deferred to a later check, because by the time anyone checks, the original file
is already gone.

### 6.2.8 The process record — errors, retractions, and a rule catching a real failure

`PLAN.md` §8 records **`6`** process errors and `PLAN.md` §9 **`5`** retracted findings — both
`[config]`, both counted by reading the tables in `PLAN.md`, LF-normalised sha256
`5a53ca781f25fb652132f92b87e35190ae84236ec3cb69b9b160a7baf56edd6b` — in the document a next reader
opens first rather than in an appendix. They are not code bugs. Three of the six are the
same error: **an unexamined assumption about our own capacity or constraints, carried unverified
across several decisions** — an eleven-day schedule built on an unasked question, a compute budget
set on an unchecked quota assumption, and, most expensively, a whole evening's cut points derived
from a deadline nobody had confirmed, which dropped an arm **nineteen hours** early
(`STAGE2_WORKPLAN_2026-08-09.md` §7g, sha256 `cfbd14a1…`, `[config]`). That third instance
happened *inside the plan that quotes the rule against it*. The retractions include claims that had
already shaped decisions before they were withdrawn.

**The pattern the record makes visible is the point.** The recurring failure on this project was
never arithmetic; it was a premise inherited from a document instead of re-derived from disk. The
standing order that answers it — *re-read the evidence before writing a number, even evidence you
gathered yourself* — is the reason this section was written from the files rather than from the
handoff that summarised them, and the reason two of that handoff's own figures did not survive the
check.

**The clearest instance is Amendment A2, and it is a retraction of a premise, not of a number.** The
fact sheet's §6 excludes the arm64 record from σ on the stated ground that it was *"produced on a
machine that no longer exists."* **The machine exists.** Its Stage-1 checkpoints and prediction
arrays had been on disk untouched since 2026-07-30; they were invisible to every audit conducted from
git because `.gitignore` excludes `TQNet/results/` wholesale. The premise had already propagated:
`docs/STATUS.md` G2 declared half of that gap *"cannot close, ever"* on the same reasoning, and
`tools/horizon_sigma.py` still prints the phrase in its own generated prose. **The exclusion itself
stands and A2 does not disturb it** — mixing architectures under a σ of ~0.002 is bad practice whether
or not the second machine is reachable — but the recorded *reason* was wrong, and a reason is what a
reader checks. The record is excluded by protocol, not by loss.

**T15′ caught a real failure, and that is a finding rather than housekeeping.** The rule — a number
that cannot be traced to a named result file, by path and sha256, does not get printed — was written
as a discipline and then bit twice. It bars Arm A's mechanism figures, which exist in two narrative
documents and in no result file, so this report can state *why* Arm A fails only qualitatively
(§6.2.0). And it is the rule that flagged the seed-spread and ablation numbers whose machine was
believed lost before their run records were committed: marked, retained, used for nothing, and then —
when the premise turned out to be false — closed by ingesting the two missing records rather than by
relaxing the rule. A traceability rule that never blocks anything is decoration. This one blocked
content the authors wanted to print, twice, and the second block was resolved by producing the
evidence.

**One disclosed, uncorrected defect in a source document.** Fact sheet §6.2 attributes the paper's
Table 5 to `PTQNet.pdf` p. 15. That is wrong. **Table 5 is in `files/project/TQnet.pdf` p. 15** —
verified for this section by reading the page, which carries *"Table 5: Full multivariate time series
forecasting results of Table 2 for all four prediction horizons"* — and PTQNet is separate, later
work: Xun et al., *Periodic-temporal query network for long-term multivariate time series
forecasting*, **Information Processing & Management 63(7):104785, 2026**. The error is flagged in the
fact sheet's own amendment block and **deliberately not edited**, because §§0–11 have a single writer
and an amendment to a sole-writer file is raised for that writer's approval, not adopted unilaterally
by a third party mid-report. `report/F5.md` carries the correct citation and sources no value from
the contested row. **This is a disclosed defect in a source document, not an error in this report** —
and the reason it is still there is a rule working, not a rule failing.

## 6.3 What we learned

**Choose the cell before you choose the method.** The single fact that determines what this study
could conclude was fixed before any modification was designed: a noise floor of
`0.0021541981747125473` `[test]` (class 1, §5) against a headline effect of `0.004` `[test]`
(class 3, §1) — **`1.8568393785468478`** σ (class 2, §6.2.3). Four modifications were then proposed
to a cell that could not resolve most of them. Nothing about the arms was wrong; the resolution was
decided in advance by the dataset, the horizon and the seed count, and no amount of care downstream
recovers it. The lesson is not "use more seeds" — it is that **the achievable resolution is a
property of the experimental cell and should be computed before the hypotheses are written**, because
it determines which hypotheses are worth writing.

**A null is only reportable if it was predicted.** Arm B's flat W-curve and the ablation agree, and
the agreement is evidence rather than a story for exactly one reason: §3 registered the conjunction
in advance, with its mechanism, and pre-declared the opposite outcome as *"a more interesting finding
still."* Had the same two results arrived unregistered, no honest reading could distinguish them from
a pattern found by looking. **Pre-registration is what converts an absence of effect from a
disappointment into a measurement** — and it is why this report has a strongest result at all.

**A threshold in units of σ is not a threshold until the split and the metric are named.** §4.4 fixed
*"more than 1σ"* and parenthesised a single number. That number turned out to be the test-split MSE σ,
while §4.1's endpoint was validation, and the two differ by a factor of
**`1.8869742103263096`** (class 2, D18); separately, σ(MSE) and
σ(MAE) move in **opposite directions** across the H = 96 → 192 step (§6.2.3). A rule stated as "1σ"
was therefore four different rules depending on how it was read. It survived only because the choice
turned out to be outcome-neutral across every verdict — checked, not assumed — and had it not been,
the study would have had a threshold to settle after seeing the results, which is the failure the
whole design exists to prevent. **Freeze the denominator with the numerator.**

**Identify artefacts by content, and know which digest you mean.** Two lessons converged on the same
point from opposite directions. The checkpoint incident (§6.2.7) showed that a **metric match is not
a hash match**: two models with different weights printed a bit-identical MSE, and a repair was
accepted on an eyeballed loss trajectory against the wrong reference. The CRLF finding (§6.2.6)
showed that even a hash is not an identity unless you say which hash: 236 of 265 tracked files carry
two different sha256 values depending on the platform that checked them out, and the fact sheet
records both kinds without distinguishing them. The rule that survives both is narrow and mechanical:
**hash the bytes, hash them before and after, and pin the platform-independent form** — the git blob
hash, or an explicitly LF-normalised digest.

**Retain superseded artefacts, marked, and accept that they stay dangerous.** The epoch-3 checkpoint
and the poisoned `_summary.json` are still on disk, and the poisoned roll-up is still the most
authoritative-looking file in the results tree. Keeping them is right — they are the evidence for
§6.2.7 and a worked example of why the traceability rule exists — but marking is not containment, and
the residual hazard is real enough that four separate documents carry an explicit bar against
sourcing from them. **The honest form of "we retain superseded artefacts" is "we retain them and they
remain hazards", not "we retain them and they are harmless."**

**Write down what you were wrong about, in the document people open first.** The retraction that
mattered most on this project (§6.2.8) was of a *premise*, not a number: a machine believed lost was
never gone, and the belief had already propagated into a status document and into a tool's generated
prose before anyone checked the disk. Errors recorded in `PLAN.md` §8 were caught by later readers
precisely because they were written down; the ones that recurred were the ones inherited as
background assumptions rather than stated as claims. **An assumption you never wrote down cannot be
retracted, and it will be inherited.**

**One writer per file, and amendments raised rather than adopted.** This section reports a wrong
citation in a frozen document and does not fix it (§6.2.8). That looks like pedantry and is not: the
alternative is a report in which any author may edit any other author's frozen source, at which point
no digest recorded anywhere means what it says. The cost is one visible, disclosed defect. The
benefit is that every other number in this report can be checked against a file whose writer is
known.

**Finally — the finding this project would have suppressed if it had been designed differently.** The
four-arm screen selected, as its improved column, an arm whose contribution is a procedure for
deriving a hyperparameter that was already correct, producing a model numerically identical to the
baseline it was screened against. One arm was abandoned at its own gate. One was never opened. One
had a registered null broken by an improvement it may not be credited for, on a split it was not
selected on. **That is the result, it required no rescue, and the pre-registration is the reason it
could be written down instead of worked around.**
