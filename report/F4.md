# F4 — Improved architecture: what we changed and why

**Every number in this section is quoted from `report/factsheet.md` and from nowhere else.** Each
carries its split label — `[validation]`, `[test]`, `[training]` or `[config]` — and the fact sheet
section it comes from is named at the point of use. No number in this section is sourced from
`results/validation/_summary.json`, which carries a superseded value (fact sheet §2).

---

## 4.1 What was changed, stated once and plainly

The modification was not a single edit to the model. It was **a four-arm screen, pre-registered in
full before any of it ran**, from which one arm was selected by a rule fixed in advance.

Four modifications to TQNet were declared, each with its mechanism, its derivation, its code change,
a quantitative prediction, and a condition under which it would be abandoned:

| Arm | What it modifies in the architecture | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | The instance-normalisation block — removes a per-window linear trend before normalising and adds it back damped on the output | Abandoned at its registered H = 96 gate |
| **B** | The source of the period hyperparameter *W* — replaces the hand-set `--cycle 24` with an estimator over training rows | **Selected** as the F5 "improved" column |
| **C** | The output head and the loss — three quantiles with pinball loss instead of one point and MSE | Never implemented; the declared drop |
| **D** | The Temporal Query and the channel-attention layer — both switched off by a criterion computed on the training split | Ran at all four horizons; not selected |

F4 explains the design, not only the winner. Three of these four arms are the study's "what did not
work", and under the pre-registration each was reportable whichever way it fell.

---

## 4.2 Why a pre-registration with a fixed selection rule, and why that is the load-bearing part

`PLAN.md` §6 allows **one** improvement, explicitly to prevent running a second one after seeing the
first one's results. Registering four arms therefore needs a defence rather than a gloss.

The failure mode PLAN §6 protects against is one of **ordering**, not of count: run an idea, find it
null, quietly run another, and report the survivor as though it had been the plan. A screen in which
**all four arms, all four predictions, and the selection rule are fixed in writing before any of
them runs** does not have that failure mode, because every arm's result is reportable whichever way
it falls and none of them can be un-declared afterwards.

But the trap the design creates is real and is named here because a reader will look for it. With
four arms and a noise floor of σ = `0.0021541981747125473` `[test]` at H = 96 (fact sheet §5),
picking whichever arm happens to score best **on the test split** is not a finding — it is a maximum
over four noisy draws, and its expected value beats any individual arm's true value even if all four
arms are worthless. It is also exactly the tuning-on-future-data that the brief's only PASS/FAIL
requirement forbids.

What makes the screen legitimate rather than a fishing expedition is therefore not the count of arms
but §4 of `report/prereg-improvement.md`, which fixes the selection rule in advance:

1. Primary endpoint: **validation** MSE at H = 96, averaged over seeds 2024/2025/2026 — validation,
   not test; mean of three seeds, not best-of.
2. The arm with the lowest mean validation MSE becomes the "improved" column. Ties inside 0.0005 are
   broken by the pre-stated order **A > B > D > C**.
3. Test is then read **once per arm per horizon**, and every arm's test result is reported — the
   selected arm in F5, the other three in F6. Nothing is run and discarded; full disclosure of all
   four is what removes the multiplicity problem, and hiding three would reintroduce it.
4. A "win" requires beating the reconstruction by more than 1σ **on the metric and horizon the arm's
   own prediction named**. Anything smaller is "no measurable effect", not a small improvement.
5. Selecting by test MSE is forbidden, and the pre-registration is the evidence if it happens.

**The commit is the evidence.** `report/prereg-improvement.md` was committed at **`ac426c7`**
("Pre-registration for the Stage 2 improvement (T13', D10)"), and every arm ran afterwards: the
Gate 3–4 measurement commit `aee6229` is a descendant of it. The document also states its own
amendment procedure — nothing is edited afterwards; corrections go in a dated `## Amendments` block
that says what changed and why. It carries exactly one such block, correcting Arm D's stated
parameter count (fact sheet §3.5). No prediction, threshold or selection rule was altered at any
point.

*(`ac426c7` and `aee6229` are repository state, read read-only; they are commit identifiers, not
measured quantities, and are the one class of identifier in this section that is not a fact-sheet
row.)*

## 4.3 Arm A — damped-trend instance normalisation

**What it modifies.** TQNet's instance normalisation subtracts each input window's mean and divides
by its standard deviation, then reverses that on the output. This removes the window's **level**. It
does not remove the window's **trend**. Arm A fits a least-squares line per window per channel on a
fixed 0..95 index, subtracts it, normalises the residual as now, and on the output de-normalises as
now and adds the trend back **damped**:

```
trend added back at step h  =  slope · Σ_{k=1..h} φ^k        0 < φ ≤ 1
```

φ = 1 recovers plain linear extrapolation; φ → 0 recovers the current model. Damping is the
classical remedy — damped Holt — for the instability of extrapolating a line fitted on 96 points out
to 720 steps, 7.5× the fitting window. The change is ~15 lines in `TQNet.forward` and **changes no
parameter count**: Arm A's `n_params` is `661640` `[config]` at both φ actually run, identical to the
reconstruction (fact sheet §7).

**Why it was worth trying.** Instance normalisation is the model's only defence against
non-stationarity, ETTh1 is a two-year sensor series with genuine drift, and a window on a clear
slope is handed to the network with the slope intact. That is cheap at H = 96 and expensive at
H = 720. The registered prediction was therefore a **shape**, not a level: null at H = 96, growing
improvement at longer horizons. A uniform improvement across all four horizons would have
*disconfirmed* the stated mechanism even if the numbers looked good.

**What happened.** The arm degraded the model decisively at the registered H = 96 gate, on
`[validation]`, seed 2024, against the reconstruction anchor `0.6712632722155959` `[validation]`
(fact sheet §7):

| φ | Arm A val_MSE `[validation]` | Δ vs reconstruction `[validation]` | Δ / σ_validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 | `0.6952801971213933` | `+0.024016924905797432` | **`+21.037673525385248`** |
| 1.0 | `0.9491608951697321` | `+0.2778976229541362` | **`+243.42498001392693`** |

Against the registered ±0.002 null band the two deltas are `12.0` and `139` times the band (fact
sheet §7, both `[validation]`). The registered abandon condition — "H = 96 degrades by more than
1σ" — fired, and the arm stopped there; φ = 0.9 and φ = 0.95 were never run and no φ was ever
frozen, so Arm A never reached the §4.1 endpoint and is not ranked. The abandonment is robust to
which reconstruction anchor is used: recomputed against the epoch-3 artefact it reads `+7.29`
σ_validation and against the clean `reconstruction_v2` retrain `+21.04` σ_validation (fact sheet §7),
so all three anchors clear the 1σ threshold by one to two orders of magnitude. The arm was also
abandoned on a *corrected* implementation, not a broken one — a real origin bug (the level carried
from the window centre while the damped trend projected from the window end) was found and fixed
before the measurement that abandoned it.

### The crowded-literature caveat — stated plainly

**Adaptive and learned normalisation for non-stationary forecasting is a busy field.**
Non-stationary Transformers, Dish-TS and SAN are all prior work aimed at the same problem Arm A
addresses, and all of them are more general than what Arm A does. Arm A is **a justified
modification to this model, not a contribution against that literature.** It was registered as such
before it ran, and it is reported as such here.

Concretely, what this caveat means for how Arm A should be read: nothing about Arm A's outcome is
evidence about that literature. Arm A failing does not mean adaptive normalisation fails on ETTh1 —
it means one particular, deliberately simple, non-learned variant (a per-window least-squares slope
re-added under geometric damping) fails on ETTh1 at H = 96. The methods named above learn their
normalisation statistics; Arm A fits a closed-form line and damps it with a hyperparameter chosen
from a four-point grid. The claim on the table was only ever "this is a defensible thing to change
about *this* architecture, given that instance norm is its only non-stationarity mechanism", and
that claim survives the arm's failure while carrying no weight against the field.

**One thing F4 cannot say.** The mechanism-level explanation of *why* Arm A fails — that the
in-window linear slope carries almost none of the within-window variance and is mean-reverting, so
projecting it forward is worse than projecting nothing — exists in this project only as prose. The
two figures that would support it (`λ̂ = −0.34`; median `4.0%` of within-window variance explained)
appear in `STAGE2_WORKPLAN_2026-08-09.md` and `PM_HANDOFF_2026-08-10_1730.md` and **in no result
file**, and are barred from the report under standing order T15′ (fact sheet, `## Withheld under
T15′`, entries W1 and W2). The qualitative claim may be stated and is stated above; the numbers are
not printed here. Arm A's *finding* does not depend on them — the abandonment rests on the
`+21.04σ` / `+243.43σ` degradations, which are fully traceable — so what is blocked is the
explanation, not the result.

## 4.4 Arm B — estimate the period *W* from the training split *(the selected arm)*

**What it modifies.** TQNet's Temporal Query is indexed end to end by a single integer, `--cycle 24`,
typed by a human, one per dataset. Arm B replaces the constant with an estimator computed on
**training rows `[0, 8640)` only** — an ACF peak and a periodogram argmax that must agree under
strict integer equality, harmonics and subharmonics counting as disagreement — plus a `--cycle auto`
path. About 40 lines.

**Why.** This is the paper's own first conceded limitation: TQNet relies on the data's inherent
periodicity to fix W, which may limit generalisation to datasets without clear periodic patterns.
Misspecification is *actively harmful* rather than merely suboptimal — the paper's own Figure 6 shows
W = 167 on Electricity scoring worse than using no Temporal Query at all. Replacing a hand-set
constant with a quantity estimated from data is an applicability improvement, and estimating it on
training rows only ties it directly to the leakage requirement.

**The deliverable was designed to be the curve, not the estimate.** The pre-registration states in
advance that on ETTh1 the estimator would almost certainly return 24, making the model numerically
identical to the reconstruction — so the reportable artefact was declared to be the **W-sensitivity
curve the authors never ran on any ETT dataset**: train at W ∈ {6, 8, 12, 23, 24, 25, 48, 168} and
plot validation MSE against W.

**What happened — all three registered predictions confirmed** (fact sheet §4):

- Both estimators returned **`24`** `[training]` independently: ACF largest local maximum at lag 24
  with `ac[24] = 0.8851540915365546` `[training]`, periodogram argmax at period 24 with power
  `19641471.641798608` `[training]`, `agree = True`. The abandon condition — "ACF and periodogram
  disagree" — did not fire.
- MSE at the estimated W equals the reconstruction to `2.321674e-10` `[validation]` via the
  independent `--cycle auto` run, inside the registered ±0.0005 band by roughly 6.5 orders of
  magnitude; on the shared W = 24 checkpoints, which Arm B re-evaluates rather than retrains, the
  difference is `0` exactly `[validation]` (fact sheet §4.4).
- **The W-curve is flat.** The entire spread across the eight per-W three-seed means is
  `0.0015267984642975962` `[validation]`, against a registered ±2σ band of
  `0.0022832301182749365` `[validation]` — a ratio of `0.6687010880231152`, so every pair of the
  eight sits inside the band (fact sheet §4.3). Eight values of W spanning a 28-fold range and
  straddling the true diurnal period move the model less than 2σ.

That third prediction is the interesting one **and it is a prediction of a null**, registered in
advance with its own reasoning: if the Temporal Query contributes nothing measurable at C = 7, then
nothing indexed by W can matter either, so W-sensitivity must vanish. If the curve were *not* flat,
the ablation conclusion would be wrong, and that was pre-declared as the more interesting finding
still. Either outcome was reportable, which is why this arm was the safe one.

**What Arm B's selection means, and does not mean.** Arm B's three-seed mean validation MSE at
H = 96 is `0.6724990175677814` `[validation]`, the lowest of the arms that reached the endpoint, so
the rule selects it (fact sheet §1.3). But its estimator returns 24, which is what was already
hard-coded, so the `--cycle auto` path produces an architecturally identical model —
`n_params = 661640` `[config]`, the reconstruction's own count (fact sheet §1.4). **The selected
"improved" column is numerically the reconstruction**, and the pre-registration separately denies it
a "win", because its own prediction named *equality* rather than improvement. F5's table and F6
carry that; it is recorded here because it is the direct consequence of the design F4 is explaining.

**One registered blocker, discharged.** The pre-registration required that PTQNet (Xun et al.,
*Information Processing & Management* 63(7):104785, doi `10.1016/j.ipm.2026.104785`) — a direct
follow-up aimed at exactly this limitation — be obtained and cited before this arm ran, and required
that a failure to obtain it be stated in F4 and F7 rather than passed over. The paper was obtained
(`PTQNet.pdf` is in the project); the citation is F7's.

## 4.5 Arm C — quantile output head with pinball loss *(never opened)*

**What it would have modified, and why.** The last layer is one `Linear(512 → H)` and the loss is
`nn.MSELoss`: one number per channel per future step, with no interval, no quantile and no variance
of any kind. Arm C would have emitted `pred_len × 3` for quantiles {0.1, 0.5, 0.9} under a pinball
loss, reporting the 0.5 quantile as the point forecast so that the four required metrics stayed
computable on the identical split. It is an applicability gain in the plainest sense — a capability
the model does not have. **Its prediction was also deliberately unflattering:** pinball loss at
τ = 0.5 optimises the conditional median while MSE optimises the conditional mean, so the registered
prediction was that MSE at H = 96 would **degrade** by 0.002–0.008 while MAE and MdAE improved — and
that an MSE *improvement* should be read as an implementation bug. Registering in advance that a
required headline metric will get worse is stronger evidence of understanding than any accuracy win
this cell can produce.

**Why it was not run, and why that is not a hole.** Arm C was the most code of the four arms and was
placed **last in an implementation order fixed in advance**, cheapest-per-unit-evidence first:
D, A, B, C. The pre-registration names it as the declared drop and specifies the exact sentence to be
used if the clock ran out — which it did, at its go/no-go gate. **A dropped arm that was declared
droppable in advance is not a missing result; an undeclared drop would be.** No measurement of
Arm C exists — no run, no checkpoint, no sidecar, no run record — so all four of its registered
predictions stand as `unevaluable`, not as nulls, and the arm remains implementable exactly as
registered.

## 4.6 Arm D — channel-count-conditional attention

**What it modifies.** The Temporal Query and the channel-attention layer always run, at C = 7 and at
C = 883 alike; attention over channels is quadratic in C, and the paper concedes its efficiency
claim is empirical and bounded to C < 1000. Arm D makes the model decide **for itself** whether to
run them, from a statistic computed on the **training split only** — the mean absolute off-diagonal
mass of the train-split channel correlation matrix — against a threshold fixed in advance. About
10 lines: the `--use_tq` / `--channel_aggre` flags already existed from the ablation, so the arm is a
criterion plus a dispatch.

**Why.** We had *measured* that both mechanisms are inert here: at C = 7 the attention map is 7×7,
and the Stage-1 ablation's three variants sit inside 1σ of each other. Spending parameters and a quadratic
operation on a mechanism contributing nothing measurable is a real cost. The improvement is not the
deletion — it is that the deletion is **conditioned on a data statistic instead of on a human
knowing the answer**.

**What happened** (fact sheet §3.6, §3.5, §3.1):

- The criterion fired: `mean_abs_offdiag_pearson_correlation = 0.3110131176996824` `[training]` on
  rows `[0, 8640)`, against `CHANNEL_CORR_DROP_THRESHOLD = 0.3` `[config]` → decision `drop`
  (`use_tq=0`, `channel_aggre=0`). Registered prediction: that it fires. It fired.
- Parameters `661640` → `624224` `[config]` at H = 96 — `37416` removed, `5.655038994014872` %
  `[config]`. Registered prediction: 661,640 → 624,224 (−5.7%). Confirmed.
- MSE within ±1σ of the reconstruction, `[test]`, at three of four horizons: Δ/σ = `−0.331` at
  H = 96, `+0.619` at H = 336, `+0.020` at H = 720. At **H = 192 the registered null is falsified**,
  Δ = `−0.0027151763117891914` `[test]` against the smallest of the four per-horizon σ, giving
  Δ/σ = `−3.241` `[test]` — broken in the *favourable* direction, by the smaller model being better.
- The efficiency prediction ("training wall-clock ≥ 10% faster") is `unevaluable`: twelve Arm D
  wall-clock values exist `[config]` but **no reconstruction wall-clock was ever recorded, at any
  horizon, in any file**, so a relative claim has no baseline. The Arm D timings are additionally
  incoherent as a compute measure — H = 96 averages `242.33…` s while H = 336 averages `97.66…` s
  `[config]` — which reflects early-stopping epoch counts and machine load, not architecture cost
  (fact sheet §3.7).

### The improvement-by-deletion objection

**Arm D improves the model by removing the paper's headline mechanism.** A reader is entitled to
ask whether that is an improvement to the architecture at all, or whether it is evidence that the
architecture was oversized for a 7-channel dataset. The objection was registered in the
pre-registration before the arm ran, precisely so that it would have to be argued in F4 rather than
assumed, and it is answered here rather than deflected.

**First, the part of the objection that is correct, conceded without qualification.** The evidence
Arm D rests on is evidence about **ETTh1 at C = 7**, and it is not evidence about the architecture
in general. The mechanism the criterion switches off is quadratic in the channel count; at C = 7 the
attention map is 7×7, which is close to the smallest non-trivial case the mechanism can be given.
Nothing measured in this study licenses the claim that the Temporal Query or the channel-attention
layer are useless on the high-channel-count datasets where the paper makes its case, and this report
does not make that claim anywhere. On this dataset, the reading "the architecture was oversized for seven
channels" is not merely admissible — **it is the better-supported reading of the two**, and it is
what the surrounding results say: three horizons inside ±1σ `[test]`, one horizon falsified in the
favourable direction, and a flat W-curve `[validation]` over a 28-fold range of the hyperparameter
that indexes the same mechanism. Two independent interventions — deleting the mechanism and sweeping
its parameter — return the same null, which is stronger evidence than either alone precisely because
each closes the other's escape route.

**Second, what that concession does and does not cost Arm D.** It costs Arm D any claim to have
improved TQNet as an architecture. It does not cost Arm D its actual content, because the arm's
contribution is not the deletion. Removing the Temporal Query and the channel-attention layer had
already been done, by hand, in Stage 1's ablation; a human looked at C = 7, decided the mechanism
was pointless, and switched it off with a flag. **Arm D's modification is the criterion that makes
that decision from a training-split statistic instead.** The architectural change is a *conditional*
where the paper has an unconditional: the model reads the channel correlation structure it was given
and configures itself. On a dataset with genuinely independent channels the criterion keeps the
mechanism; on ETTh1 it drops it. That conditionality is the thing that generalises, and it is the
thing that could be wrong — which is the subject of §4.7 below and is the arm's most serious
weakness.

**Third, the objection's sharpest form, which the above does not yet answer.** One could still say:
a criterion with one threshold, validated on exactly one dataset, that fires in the direction the
authors of the criterion expected, is not much of a conditional — it is a hand-tuned deletion with a
statistic wrapped around it. That is a fair charge and it is only partly answerable. What answers it
partly: the threshold is external (Cohen 1988), it was fixed before it was applied, the statistic is
computed on training rows only, and the prediction that it would fire was registered in writing
beforehand — so the *direction* cannot have been chosen after the fact. What does **not** answer it:
the criterion has been exercised on one dataset, at one C, and it fires by a margin small enough
that a different-but-defensible reduction would have reversed it. A criterion that has never
returned "keep" on any real data has not been tested as a conditional; it has only been observed
firing once. That is the honest limit of what Arm D demonstrates, and §4.7 states the numbers behind
it.

**The conclusion, stated so it can be disagreed with.** Arm D is an improvement in the sense the
brief permits — efficiency, on measured evidence of ours — and it is *not* an improvement to TQNet's
forecasting mechanism, because on this dataset that mechanism was not doing measurable work in the
first place. Both halves are reported. A reader who concludes from Arm D that the architecture was
oversized for a 7-channel dataset has reached the same conclusion this report reaches, and is not
disagreeing with it.

## 4.7 The criterion is marginal, and the pre-registration is why it stays that way

Arm D's gate fires at `mean_abs_offdiag_pearson_correlation = 0.3110131176996824` `[training]`,
computed on ETTh1's C = 7 channels over training rows `[0, 8640)`, against
`CHANNEL_CORR_DROP_THRESHOLD = 0.3` `[config]` (fact sheet §3.6). The margin above the threshold is
`0.0110131176996824` `[training]` (fact sheet §3.6, `[derived]`).

**What is strong about this.** The threshold is Cohen (1988)'s conventional "medium effect" boundary
for a correlation. It is **external to this study** — not chosen by us, not chosen for this dataset,
not chosen after seeing the statistic — and that is the strongest available defence against the
charge "you picked a threshold that made your prediction come true". The statistic itself was
independently recomputed from `TQNet/dataset/ETTh1.csv` over the same training rows and reproduced
to the last digit.

**What is weak about it, stated at full strength.** The margin is **0.011**, and it is carried by
**two channel pairs out of twenty-one**. ETTh1 has 7 channels, so the correlation matrix has 21
off-diagonal pairs; the |r| distribution over those 21 spans from below 0.04 to above 0.98, and the
two highest are near-duplicate sensor pairs — HUFL/MUFL ("high/middle useful load") and HULL/MULL.
Those two pairs alone carry the mean over the line. **A median-based reduction, or a mean with the
top two pairs trimmed, would have returned "keep" — and Arm D would never have fired at all.**

That is worth stating in the strongest form available: **the criterion's verdict on this dataset is
not robust to a defensible change in how the 21 pairwise correlations are reduced to one number.**
Mean, median and trimmed mean are all reasonable reductions; the pre-registration fixed the mean,
and under the other two the arm does not run. The redundancy the mean is picking up is *real
structure in ETTh1* — HUFL/MUFL are genuinely near-duplicate measurements, not noise — so the
criterion is reading something true. But a mean over a distribution this wide is a coarse summary of
it, and a summary whose verdict flips under a reasonable alternative is a weak criterion. **ETTh1
sits near the boundary, not safely inside it.**

**This is not fixed here, and it must not be.** Moving the threshold, or switching the reduction to
median or trimmed mean, after seeing that the mean fires by 0.011 is exactly the act the
pre-registration exists to prevent: tuning a criterion to a result. The threshold was fixed before
it was applied and it stays fixed. Stating the marginality is worth more than concealing it, and it
is stated here as F4 content rather than left for a reader to discover.

**And it cuts in a direction that supports, rather than undermines, the registered result.** Arm D's
prediction was MSE **within ±1σ** — statistical indistinguishability, a registered null. A marginal
criterion firing on a marginal dataset is exactly what one expects if neither mechanism is doing
measurable work at C = 7, and it is consistent with the ablation finding and with the flat W-curve.
The weak point is the criterion's *generality*, not this study's verdict.

*Sourcing note.* The two figures printed in this subsection —
`0.3110131176996824` `[training]` and `0.3` `[config]` — and the derived margin
`0.0110131176996824` `[training]` are fact sheet §3.6 rows. The per-pair |r| values, the median and
the trimmed mean behind the qualitative claims above are recorded in
`STAGE2_WORKPLAN_2026-08-09.md` §7e and are **not** fact-sheet rows; their numeric values are
therefore not printed here, only the claims they support. The pair count 21 is C(7,2) from the
C = 7 recorded in fact sheet §3.6.

## 4.8 Arm D's efficiency claim weakens with the horizon — a measured limitation

`37,416` parameters are removed **at every horizon**. The count is horizon-independent; the
percentage is not, because the denominator grows — the output head scales with `pred_len` while the
two dropped mechanisms do not. All rows `[config]`, fact sheet §3.5:

| H | Reconstruction `n_params` | Arm D `n_params` | Removed | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96 | `661640` | `624224` | `37416` | `5.655038994014872` % |
| 192 | `710888` | `673472` | `37416` | `5.26327635295574` % |
| 336 | `784760` | `747344` | `37416` | `4.767827106376472` % |
| 720 | `981752` | `944336` | `37416` | `3.811145788345733` % |

**The absolute saving is horizon-invariant while the percentage falls monotonically, `5.66%` →
`3.81%`.** So Arm D's efficiency claim is strongest exactly where it matters least — at the shortest
horizon, where the model is smallest and cheapest anyway — and weakest at H = 720, where the paper's
own results are worst and where efficiency would be most valuable. **This is a measured limitation
of the arm, stated rather than discovered**: it falls out of the four recorded parameter counts and
was not visible from the H = 96 figure alone, which is the only one the pre-registration quoted.

The `37,416` figure itself is the subject of the pre-registration's single `## Amendments` block.
The original derivation paragraph quoted `37248` — the channel-attention block alone — while
describing a change that removes **both** mechanisms. The correct decomposition is
`37248` (channel attention) `+ 168` (Temporal Query) `= 37416` `[config]`, which matches
`661640 − 624224` directly. The amendment corrected a stated count and altered no prediction,
threshold or selection rule; `37248` appearing anywhere as Arm D's total is the corrected error, not
the count.

## 4.9 What the rule returned

Two arms reached the §4.1 endpoint — a three-seed mean validation MSE at H = 96. Arms A and C did
not, and are not ranked (fact sheet §1.3):

| Arm | Mean val_MSE at H = 96 `[validation]` | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | **`0.6724990175677814`** | **1 (lowest) — selected** |
| D | `0.6805545682661754` | 2 |

The margin is `0.008055550698394032` `[validation]`, which is `16.1`× the 0.0005 tie band, so the
tiebreak clause `A > B > D > C` is inert and never consulted — the awkward question of whether an
abandoned arm can win a tiebreak it is ranked first in therefore never arises. In units of the
validation σ the margin is `+7.056275785710372` `[validation]`.

**Arm D's `[test]` result at H = 96 is nominally better than the reconstruction and did not enter
the decision.** Arm D was ranked second on the `[validation]` endpoint alone and would have been
ranked second there if its `[test]` result had never been computed. Featuring an arm because it did
better on test is the one form of reasoning the pre-registration declares fatal to the study.

The rule, applied without discretion, therefore selected the arm that **changes nothing** — and the
pre-registration separately classifies it as "no measurable effect", because Arm B's own prediction
named equality. That outcome was pre-declared as coherent rather than as a failure: *all four arms
null, and the W-curve flat*, is the shape the design was built to be able to report without rescue.
