# Platform replication -- the Stage-2 arms on macOS/arm64

Generated by `tools/platform_compare.py`. Every Stage-2 number in the report was
measured on x86. This file re-measures the three live arms on the arm64 machine that
`docs/STATUS.md` G2 recorded as unavailable, to separate **platform** from **seed** in
the sigma every Stage-2 verdict is judged against.

**This is not a new arm and it changes no pre-registered prediction.** The selection
rule (`report/prereg-improvement.md` sec 4) was applied before any run here existed.
Post-hoc by construction; F6 content.

Records live in `results/platform-arm64/runs/`, deliberately outside `results/runs/`,
so a platform replication cannot be mistaken for a seed, displace the x86 re-baseline
in the F5 table, or reach the sigma `tools/horizon_sigma.py` computes.

> x86 Arm B seed 2024 validation was sourced from `results/validation/validation_metrics.log` (0.6712632722155959), not from its sidecar, which holds the epoch-3 artefact 0.6869550701723053 left by the incident recorded in `w_curve_correction.log`.

## Where the non-determinism actually lives

`CLAUDE.md` records that CPU training here is not bit-reproducible under a fixed
seed. That is correct, but it is not uniform across configurations, and the
difference is worth having on record because the incident in that file turned on it.
Each group below is the *same* configuration -- ETTh1, L=H=96, cycle 24, seed 2024 --
trained more than once on this machine, under a byte-identical environment
(torch 2.9.1, numpy 2.2.6, 10 threads) and with no change to the attention path in
`TQNet/models/TQNet.py` between the dates shown.

### full model (attention in the graph)

|---|---|---|

### Why this matters more than it looks

The attention path is the only structural difference between the two groups, and it
is the one that fails to reproduce. A per-element prediction difference of order
1e-06 is invisible in a float32 metric accumulated over 1,871,520 elements -- the
full-model runs above print a **bit-identical** `mse:0.3710499405860901` from
`TQNet/utils/metrics.py` while having different weights and different predictions --
and shows up only around the tenth significant figure of a float64 metric.

That is exactly the signature the incident in `CLAUDE.md` sec 'Never overwrite data'
describes: a repaired checkpoint whose weights hashed differently and whose `val_MSE`
differed at the tenth significant figure. This section is the direct demonstration
that such a difference is what an ordinary attention-path re-run looks like, and is
not by itself evidence that anything was misconfigured.

It is also the strongest available argument for that file's central rule. Two runs
here produced an identical printed MSE from different models. **A metric match is not
a hash match** -- not as a matter of principle, but demonstrated, on this data, with
the very metric that would have been used to check.
