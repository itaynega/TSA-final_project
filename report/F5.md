## 5.2 The improved column equals the reconstruction column, and why

**This is the table's central fact and it is not a formatting error.**

Arm B replaces the hand-typed `--cycle 24` with an estimator run on training rows `[0, 8640)`. On
ETTh1 **the estimator returns `24`** — by ACF peak and by periodogram argmax independently, with
strict integer agreement (fact sheet §4.1, `[training]`, class 1). `24` is exactly the value the
reconstruction already had hard-coded. The `--cycle auto` path therefore resolves to a
byte-identical setting string and an architecturally identical model — `n_params = 661640`
`[config]` on both sides (fact sheet §1.4, class 1).

**Consequently Arm B's W = 24 row is not a retrain.** Fact sheet §1.4: it *"re-evaluates the
reconstruction's own three checkpoints."* Same weights, same evaluator, same split → the same
number. **Δ (improved − reconstruction) is `0` at every horizon, for both metrics, and
Δ/σ is `0` against each of the eight σ in §5.1** — class 2, derived by subtracting each
reconstruction cell from the identical improved cell.

Two measured corroborations exist, both at H = 96 and both on `[validation]`, and **neither is a
`[test]` quantity — they are labelled here and do not appear in the §5.1 table:**

- **`0`** (exact) — the difference on the shared W = 24 checkpoints, `[validation]` (fact sheet
  §4.4, class 1).
- **`2.321674e-10`** `[validation]` — the difference against an *independent* end-to-end
  `--cycle auto` retrain, i.e. the estimator run from scratch rather than assumed (fact sheet §4.4,
  class 1). This is roughly seven orders of magnitude below the `[validation]` σ of
  `0.0011416150591374683` (fact sheet §1.1, class 1), and inside the pre-registration's declared
  **`±0.0005`** equality band by about six and a half orders of magnitude. The `±0.0005` is
  **class 3 — declared**, from Arm B's §3 prediction (*"MSE at estimated W equals the reconstruction
  to within ±0.0005 (it is the same model)"*), not measured here.

**At H = 192, 336 and 720 the identity holds by construction, not by a separate test-split
measurement.** The estimator runs on the input series over training rows `[0, 8640)` and is
horizon-independent; W = 24 resolves the same at every horizon, so the improved model *is* the
reconstruction model at every horizon and its test metrics are the same numbers. Stated plainly so
the table is not over-read: **the improved column at H = 192/336/720 reproduces the reconstruction
column because it is the same model, and no additional `[test]` evaluation was run to produce
those three pairs.**

## 5.3 Why two σ per row, and what a single σ would have done

σ is not a constant of this study and not a smooth function of the horizon. Both statements below
are class 2, derived from the eight class-1 σ in §5.1:

- **σ(MSE) dips at H = 192; σ(MAE) rises there.** σ(MSE) falls from `0.0021541981747125473` at
  H = 96 to `0.0008376947093460351` at H = 192 — a factor of **`2.571579061773316`** *down* — while
  over the same step σ(MAE) climbs from `0.0003752412844557897` to `0.0015920913669081958`, a factor
  of **`4.242847023661580`** *up*. The two move in opposite directions across the same horizon step.
- **The ordering of the two σ inverts between adjacent horizons.** At H = 96, σ(MSE) is
  **`5.740834668116992`** × σ(MAE). At H = 192, σ(MSE) is only **`0.5261599470718936`** × σ(MAE) —
  equivalently σ(MAE) is **`1.900562757703337`** × σ(MSE). At H = 336 the ratio σ(MSE)/σ(MAE) is
  **`3.499099031573422`**, at H = 720 it is **`1.531273392430224`**.

**The concrete failure a single-σ table would produce.** Suppose an MAE difference at H = 192 landed
anywhere between `0.0008376947093460351` and `0.0015920913669081958` — that is, between one σ(MSE)
and one σ(MAE) at that horizon. Judged against σ(MSE), it clears the pre-registration's declared
**`±1σ`** win threshold and reads as a **win**. Judged against σ(MAE) — the σ that actually belongs
to the metric — it is inside `±1σ` and reads as **"no measurable effect."** The two verdicts are
opposites, the interval is not narrow, and it exists at H = 192 precisely because that is where the
two σ cross. `±1σ` is **class 3 — declared**, from pre-registration §4 item 4 (*"A 'win' requires the
improvement to beat the reconstruction by more than 1σ on the metric and horizon its own §3
prediction named"*).

**The horizon range is large enough that reusing any one σ everywhere fails too.** σ(MSE) at H = 720
is **`10.56963052716745`** × its value at H = 96, and σ(MAE) at H = 720 is
**`39.62617104137760`** × its value at H = 96 (class 2). As a fraction of its own mean, σ(MSE) grows
from **`0.5780499733526942`** % at H = 96 to **`4.517676738541180`** % at H = 720 (class 2). This is
the same non-monotonicity fact sheet §5 records; F5 restates it because the table is where a reader
would otherwise assume a single noise floor.

**A note on the declared σ.** Pre-registration §4 item 4 writes *"At σ = 0.00215, a 0.001 gain is not
a gain."* Both **`0.00215`** and **`0.001`** there are **class 3 — declared**, fixed before any run.
They are borne out: the measured σ(MSE) at H = 96 is `0.0021541981747125473` `[test]` (class 1),
which exceeds the declared `0.00215` by **`0.0000041981747125473`** (class 2), and the declared
`0.001` gain would be **`0.4642098446367119`** measured σ (class 2) — under half a σ, exactly as the
pre-registration anticipated.

## 5.5 What the table shows

**Read across, there is no effect to report.** The improved column reproduces the reconstruction
column exactly at all four horizons and on both metrics; Δ = `0` and Δ/σ = `0` everywhere (class 2,
§5.2). Under the pre-registration's declared **`±1σ`** rule this is **"no measurable effect"** — and
Arm B is the one arm for which that verdict is a *confirmation* rather than a disappointment,
because its own §3 prediction named equality (*"it is the same model"*) rather than improvement.

**The arm's real deliverable is not in this table.** Arm B was registered to produce the
W-sensitivity curve the authors never ran on any ETT dataset. That curve is flat: the spread across
the eight per-W means is `0.0015267984642975962` `[validation]`, which is
`0.6687010880231152` of the declared **`±2σ`** band (fact sheet §4.3, both class 1; `±2σ` class 3),
so every pair of the eight sits inside it. **That is a measured null, pre-registered as a null, and
it is the arm's result** — F4 §4.4 and F6 carry it. F5's table cannot show it, because a table of
one selected setting has nowhere to put a sensitivity curve.

## 5.6 The selected arm changes nothing, and that is the finding

**Stated without softening, because the table above is otherwise unreadable.**

The pre-registered rule was applied without discretion and it selected Arm B. Fact sheet §1.3 and
`report/selection.md` §1.4: Arm B's mean validation MSE at H = 96, `0.6724990175677814`
`[validation]` (class 1), is the lowest among the arms that reached the §4.1 endpoint; Arm D's is
`0.6805545682661754` `[validation]` (class 1). The margin, `0.008055550698394032` `[validation]`,
is **`16.1`** × the declared **`0.0005`** tie band (fact sheet §1.3; the margin and the `16.1` are
class 1 as fact-sheet `[derived]` rows with their derivations stated there, the `0.0005` is
class 3 — declared, from pre-registration §4 item 2), so the tiebreak clause never fires and the
preference order is never consulted.

**And §4.4 separately denies Arm B a "win."** The declared **`±1σ`** win test is applied on the
metric and horizon the arm's own §3 prediction named — and Arm B's §3 prediction named **equality**.
An arm that predicted equality and delivered equality has confirmed its prediction and won nothing.
Selection and classification are different operations on the same result: §4.2 ranks, §4.4
classifies, and the frozen text applies them independently (`report/selection.md` §1.4, ruling
(a-iii)).

So: **this study's four-arm screen selected, as its improved column, an arm whose contribution is a
procedure for deriving a hyperparameter that was already correct — producing a model numerically
identical to the baseline it was screened against.** No arm was substituted to make the table look
better. In particular, **Arm D scored better than the reconstruction on `[test]` at H = 192** —
by `−0.0027151763117891914` `[test]`, which is `−3.241` σ (fact sheet §3.1, both class 1) — **and
that is precisely the number a study invalidates itself by reaching for.** Pre-registration §4
item 5 forbids it in terms: *"Selecting by test MSE is forbidden. If any post-hoc reasoning of the
form 'arm X did better on test so let's feature it' appears, the study is invalid."* Arm D was
selected against on `[validation]`, where it is worse, and it is reported in F6 with all three
non-selected arms. **The improved column of F5 stays as the rule left it.**
